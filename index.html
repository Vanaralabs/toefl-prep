Speaking Section

4 tasks • 16 minutes total • Templates + Tips

Task 1 — Independent (Personal Opinion)

⏱️ 15 sec prep | 45 sec speak

🎯 The OREO Template (Opinion-Reason-Example-Opinion):

O: "I think [choice]. Here's why."

R: "First, [reason 1]."

E: "For example, [story/detail in 1-2 sentences]."

O: "That's why I prefer [choice]."

💡 Even simpler (if you freeze):

"I prefer [X] because [one reason]. Last year, [personal story]. So yeah, [X] is better for me."

That's it. 3 sentences = 30 seconds = enough for a good score.

🔑 Universal starter phrases (memorize these):

• "I think..." / "I prefer..." / "I'd say..."

• "The main reason is..."

• "For example, last year / when I was in college / at my job..."

• "That's why I believe..."

Task 2 — Integrated (Campus Announcement)

⏱️ 30 sec prep | 60 sec speak

🎯 The SAR Template (Situation-Attitude-Reasons):

S: "The university is going to [change]."

A: "The student [likes / doesn't like] this."

R: "First, because [reason 1 from conversation]. Also, [reason 2]."

🔑 Universal phrases:

• "The university announced that..."

• "The student agrees/disagrees because..."

• "He/She mentions that..." / "He/She also says..."

Task 3 — Integrated (Academic Concept)

⏱️ 30 sec prep | 60 sec speak

🎯 The DEX Template (Define-Example-eXplain):

D: "[Term] means [definition in your words]."

E: "The professor gives an example. He talks about [example]."

X: "This shows how [term] works."

🔑 Universal phrases:

• "The reading explains that..."

• "The professor gives an example of this..."

• "This shows / This demonstrates / This is an example of..."

Task 4 — Integrated (Lecture Summary)

⏱️ 20 sec prep | 60 sec speak

🎯 The T-T Template (Topic-Two things):

T: "The professor talks about [topic]."

1: "The first point is [point 1 + detail]."

2: "The second point is [point 2 + detail]."

🔑 Universal phrases:

• "The professor explains two types/ways/examples of..."

• "The first one is..." / "The second one is..."

• "He/She gives the example of..."

Speaking Tips & Tricks

The 3-2-1 Rule: Have 3 personal stories ready (travel, school, work). Twist any of them to fit ANY Task 1 question.

Filler phrases: "Well, from my perspective..." / "That's an interesting question..." / "I'd have to say that..."

Speed: Speak at 70% of your max speed. Slow + clear > fast + mumbling.

Transitions: "First of all" → "Secondly" → "In addition" → "Therefore" — these alone boost your score.

If you freeze: Say "Let me think about this..." then restart. Never go silent.

1-2 grammar mistakes won't hurt. Fluency + organization > perfect grammar.

Universal Starters (Memorize ONE)

Pick one. Use it EVERY time. Don't think.

• "I'd say..." ← shortest, most natural

• "Well, personally..."

• "From my experience..."

• "If I had to choose..."

❌ NEVER say:

• "I understand that you want to know..." (wastes 8 sec, AI scores it as zero content)

• "That's a really interesting question..." (filler, adds nothing)

• "There are many opinions on this topic..." (vague, no position)

Transition Phrases (Connect Your Ideas)

Between reason 1 → reason 2:

• "Also..." / "On top of that..." / "Another thing is..."

• "Plus..." / "And honestly..." / "Besides that..."

Giving examples:

• "Like, last year..." / "For example, when I..."

• "I remember once..." / "In my case..."

Wrapping up:

• "So yeah, that's why I think..."

• "That's my take." / "So for me, [choice] is better."

Emergency Phrases (When You Freeze)

If you blank out mid-sentence:

• "What I mean is..." (restart the same point)

• "Let me put it this way..." (rephrase)

• "The point is..." (jump to conclusion)

If you run out of things to say:

• Add a personal story: "I remember when I was in college..."

• Add contrast: "Some people might disagree, but for me..."

• Repeat your position: "So yeah, that's why [choice] works better."

If you don't understand the question:

• Pick the simpler option and talk about it. Don't waste time deciding.

• Say: "I think [X] is better" — even if you're unsure. Confidence > correctness.

Score Boosters (Easy Wins)

Vocabulary upgrades (replace basic words):

• good → beneficial / effective / valuable

• bad → harmful / problematic / counterproductive

• important → essential / crucial / significant

• a lot → significantly / considerably

• think → believe / consider / feel strongly that

Sound smarter with these patterns:

• "Not only [X], but also [Y]" — instant complex sentence

• "The reason I say this is because..." — shows depth

• "From a practical standpoint..." — sounds analytical

• "In the long run..." — shows big picture thinking

The 3-story bank (prepare these in advance):

• 📚 A school/college story — use for education, teamwork, learning questions

• 💼 A work/internship story — use for career, skills, challenges questions

• ✈️ A travel/hometown story — use for culture, place, lifestyle questions

Twist ANY of these to fit ANY question. "Do you prefer city or town?" → use travel story. "Is teamwork important?" → use college story.

Timing Guide (What to Say When)

Task 1 — 45 seconds breakdown:

• 0-5 sec: Starter + opinion ("I'd say I prefer X because...")

• 5-20 sec: Reason 1 + example

• 20-35 sec: Reason 2 + example (or extend reason 1)

• 35-45 sec: Wrap up ("So that's why...")

If you only have 1 reason with a good story, that's fine. One deep reason > two shallow ones.

Writing Section

2 tasks • 29 minutes total • Templates + Power Phrases

Task 1 — Integrated Writing (20 min)

Read passage → Listen to lecture → Write 250-300 words

🎯 The 1+3 Template (1 intro + 3 body paragraphs):

Intro: "The reading says [topic]. The professor disagrees."

Body 1: "The reading says [point 1]. But the professor says [counter]."

Body 2: "The reading also says [point 2]. But the professor says [counter]."

Body 3: "Finally, the reading says [point 3]. The professor says [counter]."

⚠️ NO conclusion. NO opinion. Just: reading says X → professor says Y. That's it.

🔑 Copy-paste power verbs (use instead of "says"):

Reading: claims / argues / states / suggests / mentions

Professor: disagrees / challenges / refutes / points out / explains

Linking: however / on the other hand / in contrast / but

Task 2 — Academic Discussion (10 min)

Read discussion → Add your opinion → 120-150 words

🎯 The AEE Template (Agree-Explain-Example):

A: "I agree with [name] because [1 sentence why]."

E: "I also think [your new point they didn't mention]."

E: "For example, [real story or fact in 2-3 sentences]."

Done. That's 120 words. Don't overthink.

💡 Shortcut if you're stuck:

"I agree with [A/B]. They make a good point about [X]. I'd add that [your new idea]. In my experience, [personal story]. So I believe [restate]."

^ That's literally the whole answer. 5 sentences. Done.

🔑 Universal phrases:

• "I agree with [name] because..."

• "I'd also like to add that..."

• "In my experience..." / "For example, when I..."

• "That's why I believe..."

Writing Tips & Power Phrases

Task 1 verbs: "refutes" / "casts doubt on" / "challenges" / "contradicts" / "undermines" / "disputes"

Task 1 golden rule: Don't add your opinion! Just compare reading vs. lecture. Paraphrase, don't copy.

Task 2 is short: Only 120-150 words. Pick a side FAST, give ONE strong example, done.

Vary sentences: Mix short + long. "This is crucial. Because without adequate funding, institutions cannot deliver quality education."

Save 2 min to proofread: Fix spelling, subject-verb agreement, missing articles (a/the).

Connectors: "Furthermore" / "In addition" / "On the other hand" / "As a result" / "In contrast"

Reading Section

2 passages • 10 questions each • 35 minutes total

Strategy: Don't Read Everything!

1Skim first sentence of each paragraph (30 sec)
2Go straight to questions
3Read in detail ONLY when question points to specific paragraph
4Max 2 min per question — flag and move on if stuck

Question Types

Factual Answer is directly in the passage, 1-2 sentences
Negative (EXCEPT) 3 options ARE in passage, find the odd one
Inference One logical step beyond what's written
Purpose WHY did author say this? (illustrate/contrast/support)
Vocabulary Meaning from context, not dictionary
Reference What does "they/this/such" refer to?
Insert Text Use logical connectors as clues
Summary Main ideas only — skip minor details (worth 2 pts!)

Tips

Vocabulary Q: Look at the sentence context, not the word itself. Which answer makes sense in THAT sentence?

Insert Text: Look for pronouns (this, these) and linking words (however, furthermore) — they tell you what must come BEFORE.

Inference: If you see it word-for-word in the passage, it's probably WRONG. The answer is always one step away.

Summary Q (last one): Eliminate examples and minor details. Keep only MAIN IDEAS. This is worth 2 points — spend time here.

Listening Section

3-4 lectures + 2-3 conversations • 36 minutes

Note-Taking System (T-Chart)

Left: Main Points

• Topic/thesis

• Key arguments

• Problems

Right: Details

• Examples

• Numbers/dates

• Solutions

Note-Taking Shortcuts

→ leads to / causes
↑↓ increase / decrease
≠ but / however / different
∴ therefore / so
★ important (emphasized)
? student question
ex: example follows
def: definition
vs comparison

Listening Tips

Signal words: "The main point is..." / "What's interesting is..." / "Now, this is important..." = ANSWER coming.

Tone shifts: When professor says "But..." or changes tone — that's ALWAYS tested.

Conversations: Focus on PROBLEM + SOLUTION = 60% of answers.

Lectures: Write down any comparison (X vs Y), cause-effect, or list of types. Almost always tested.

Replay: You get ONE replay per section. Save it for "attitude/tone" questions.
